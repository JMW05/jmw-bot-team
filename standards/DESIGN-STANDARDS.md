# Design Standards — Premium Without Looking Overbuilt

## Goal

A finished site should feel deliberate, brand-specific, polished, and easy to use. "Functional" is not the same as "premium."

## Evaluate

- visual hierarchy;
- typography;
- spacing rhythm;
- grid/alignment;
- color discipline;
- image quality and cropping;
- motion quality;
- CTA clarity;
- navigation clarity;
- responsive composition;
- content density;
- form experience;
- empty/error/success states;
- accessibility;
- brand consistency.

## Avoid

- generic template composition;
- random gradients or glass effects with no brand reason;
- oversized hero text that destroys mobile layout;
- excessive card grids;
- inconsistent border radii/shadows;
- too many font styles;
- filler copy and fake metrics;
- stock-looking imagery when stronger brand imagery exists;
- animation that delays access to content;
- motion that breaks with reduced-motion;
- mobile layouts that are merely shrunken desktop layouts.

## Premium test

Ask:
1. Does every major section have a clear purpose?
2. Does the visual system repeat intentionally?
3. Can a user understand the offer and next action quickly?
4. Does the experience still feel designed at 390–430px?
5. Are image crops intentional rather than accidental?
6. Are transitions smooth without becoming the product?
7. Does anything look unfinished, AI-generated, or pasted from a template?

## Review output

Score each 1–10:
- Brand/visual quality
- Hierarchy
- Typography
- Spacing/layout
- Imagery
- Motion
- Mobile experience
- UX/CTA clarity
- Accessibility
- Overall finish

Classify findings:
- P0 — broken/blocking
- P1 — materially prevents premium or correct experience
- P2 — polish/quality improvement
- P3 — optional enhancement
